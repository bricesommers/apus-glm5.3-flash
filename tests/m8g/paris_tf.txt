Paris rewards slow travel, and three days is enough to fall for it.

Start with the landmarks. The Eiffel Tower is most magical at dusk from the Champ de Mars, when the iron lattice begins to sparkle on the hour. Cross the Seine to the Louvre, where even a hurried two hours can take in the Mona Lisa and the Winged Victory, then walk the Tuileries toward the Arc de Triomphe. Save one morning for Montmartre: climb to Sacré-Cœur before the crowds, then wander the vineyard and the Place du Tertre.

Eat seriously but casually. Breakfast is a croissant and a café crème at any corner boulangerie. Lunch on a croque-monsieur or a buckwheat galette in Saint-Germain, and picnic on the Canal Saint-Martin with cheese, charcuterie, and a baguette from a market street like Rue Mouffetard. For dinner, book a bistro and order whatever the menu du jour suggests.

Getting around is simple. The Métro covers everything; buy a carnet of tickets or load a Navigo Easy card. Walk whenever you can, because the city is compact and the walks are the point.
